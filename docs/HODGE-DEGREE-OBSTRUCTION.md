# Why dense special fibers do not finish the Hodge argument

The rational Hodge conjecture asks for a rational combination of algebraic cycle classes representing each rational Hodge class on a smooth complex projective variety. For a flat class in a family, dense fibers with algebraic representatives can come from infinitely many families of cycles. The argument that would transport those representatives to all fibers needs a dominant family of cycles. The existing density result explicitly leaves that step open. [Deligne's problem statement](https://www.claymath.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/hodge.pdf) · [Dense Algebraic Loci of Hodge Classes, Theorem 1.2 and Lemma 4.2](https://hexagonmath.org/pdf/2610.00315v2)

## An exact obstruction to bounding the graphs of Hecke transports

Fix an elliptic curve $E_\tau=\mathbb C/(\mathbb Z+\tau\mathbb Z)$, with $\operatorname{Im}\tau>0$. The upper half-plane is the orbit of $\tau$ under $\mathrm{GL}_2^+(\mathbb R)$, and the rational matrices in that group are dense. Fractional linear transformations by those rational matrices give period lattices commensurable with the original one up to scaling. In particular, the curves obtained are isogenous to $E_\tau$. Mapping to the modular curve shows that the isogeny class of $E_\tau$ is countable and analytically dense, hence Zariski dense.

In contrast, for any integer $B$ there are only finitely many isogenies $E_\tau\to E'$ of degree at most $B$ up to isomorphism of the target that respects the map. An isogeny of degree $n$ factors through the quotient by its kernel, which is an order-$n$ subgroup of the finite group $E_\tau[n]$. This argument includes cyclic isogenies as a special case. For the product of the degree-one polarizations on $E_\tau$ and $E'$, the degree of an isogeny graph is exactly $1+n$: restriction of the first line bundle to the graph has degree 1, while restriction of the second has degree $n$ by pullback. Take any modular-curve point outside the isogeny class. A sequence of targets approaching that point necessarily uses graph degrees tending to infinity, since for each bound it must eventually avoid the finite set of targets below that bound.

This shows that density of transported graphs gives no uniform degree bound on those graphs. The example concerns a particular source and transport construction. It supplies no lower bound on the least degree of alternative representatives of a fixed persistent Hodge class.

## A sufficient condition worth checking

Let $X\to S$ be smooth and projective over a smooth irreducible complex algebraic variety, and let $\xi$ be a global flat rational class of degree $2p$. Suppose that on a Zariski-dense set of fibers, $\xi$ is represented by a rational combination of codimension-$p$ cycles with a fixed finite list of possible tuples of Hilbert polynomials for their reduced irreducible components, using one fixed relative polarization. In particular the number of components in the representation is bounded. The coefficients themselves may vary and need no bound.

For any tuple, form the fiber product over $S$ of the corresponding relative Hilbert schemes. It is projective and of finite type over $S$ and has finitely many connected components. The flat universal subschemes give locally constant cohomology classes along the parameter space. If a coefficient tuple works at one point of a connected component, flatness of $\xi$ and of the classes of the universal top-dimensional cycles makes that same tuple work on the entire component. The component has closed image in $S$. The finite union of images of the working components contains the dense set, so equals $S$. Each fiber then has the required rational cycle representative.

For the generic abelian families, the research obligation is to construct representatives with such uniformly bounded geometry, or to prove dominance by another method. Certifying more special fibers by itself addresses density rather than dominance. [Current scope of the linked Hodge research](https://github.com/creelie/fermat-hodge-)
